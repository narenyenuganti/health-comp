HealthComp privacy

HealthComp supports private, seven-day Activity competitions: one-on-one matches, and group matches of three or four players in which a bot can fill a seat. The initial private beta is invitation-only for a small group of adults in the United States.

Information used by the app

  • Your account: an account identifier and the display name you choose connect you to your competitions. Sign in with Apple and Supabase Auth handle sign-in. Auth account and provider identity records can also contain an email address supplied by Apple, including a private-relay address, and sign-in metadata. Your Apple email-sharing choice affects the information Apple provides.
  • Username, search and friends: you can choose a username. Show Me in Search is on until you turn it off in account settings. While you have a username and it is on, people signed in to HealthComp can find you by username or display name and see your display name, username and profile photo. The server stores your friends, the friend requests you send and receive, your choice of who can challenge you, and a count of your recent searches to limit them. If you report someone's account, the server records who reported whom and when.
  • Profile photo: if you add one, the app uploads a square JPEG of at most 512 pixels, without location or other photo metadata, to a private storage bucket, linked to your account with a version identifier. If you report someone's photo, the server records who reported whom, which photo version, and when; we may review a report and remove the photo.
  • Activity on your iPhone: HealthComp reads Activity summaries to calculate competition scores. A practice match also reads your last 2 weeks of Activity on your iPhone to set the pacer; practice data is never sent. HealthComp requests related Activity/workout read permissions to notice changes and wheelchair-use information to select Stand/Roll behavior. It does not request permission to write Health data. Raw HealthKit data stays on your iPhone; the score submission does not upload raw workout samples, routes, or raw goal values.
  • Derived competition data: our server stores rounded Activity percentages, Move/Stand/Roll modes, availability, daily points, competition dates/time zone, revision information, results, awards, and the preset reactions you send (👏 🔥 😤 🫡) and when you sent them (in a group match, also whom you sent them to), linked to participant accounts. A bot in a group match is set from the players' average daily points in earlier matches. Keeping raw Health data on-device does not mean no fitness information is collected by the service.
  • On your Apple Watch: if you install the Watch app, your iPhone sends match names, points, dates, time zones, and results, including who holds the belt, straight to your paired Watch. Health values are never sent to it.
  • Installation and security information: installation identifiers, notification tokens, which kinds of notification you turned off on that device, and app-integrity verification records support notification delivery, secure score submission, and installation retirement.
  • Operational and support information: the service keeps workflow status and error/event records to operate competitions and account cleanup. If you email support, your email address and the message you choose to send are also received. Our providers process operational information such as authentication events, IP addresses and location inferred from them, browser/device information, and website connection diagnostics. This is separate from competition records and raw HealthKit data.

We use this information to authenticate accounts, calculate and synchronize competitions, deliver notifications, protect score submissions, complete account cleanup, and investigate support requests and service failures.

Sharing and service providers

Everyone in a match with you (one other person, or up to three in a group match) can see your display name and shared competition information, including daily points, availability, dates, results, the reactions you send, rounded Move/Exercise/Stand-or-Roll percentages, Activity modes, and when your iPhone last updated each daily score. These are derived fitness values, not raw HealthKit samples or workout records. Before a group match starts, the people invited to it see each other's display names. A private invitation link grants its recipient a chance to join; share it only with your intended competitor, not in a public issue or support message. Either person can share an image of a finished match's result from the app. It shows both final scores, the week, which days each person won and up to two highlights, and the person sharing can hide the other person's name.

If you add a profile photo, it is shown to your friends, to people you sent a friend request to, to people you have or had a match with, to people invited to a group match you are in, and to someone you have a pending challenge out to. While you have a username and Show Me in Search is on, anyone signed in to HealthComp can see it. It is not shown to someone if either of you blocked the other or they reported your photo. Their apps download it with their own sign-in and keep a copy on their iPhone to show it; that copy is dropped when your photo changes or is removed and their app next refreshes, and when they sign out.

Your 1v1 career (weeks won, lost and tied, how many people you have played, belts you hold, your longest win streak and your best week's points) is shared only if you turn on Show my career. It is off until you do. While it is on, your friends and people you have or had a match with can see it on your profile, unless either of you blocked the other. Turning it off hides it right away.

Supabase provides the configured authentication and competition backend. Apple provides sign-in, notification delivery, and app-integrity services. Remote push alerts contain generic text, but routing includes a competition identifier and uses an installation's push token. This is not identifier-free traffic. The app also generates local notifications on your iPhone that can include competitor display names, points, results, and schedule reminders. Your system notification settings control previews. Cloudflare hosts the public website and invitation pages. The site's responses enable browser network-error reporting to Cloudflare; the live support response also includes Cloudflare's email-decoding script. Connection-error reports can include the requested URL and referrer, so avoid placing private information in website URLs or sharing private invitation links publicly. If you join the waitlist on the website, Resend receives the email address you enter and emails it to our Gmail inbox so we can invite you to test. Google Gmail receives mail sent to our support address, including the sender's address and message. We handle support mail in Gmail without automatic forwarding or connected helpdesk, AI, or other support-mail services.

Supabase's customer-data processing terms address its handling of participant data on our behalf. Its separate privacy notice describes the provider's own processing, not a replacement policy for HealthComp's participant records. Other provider notices describe their own practices: Apple, Cloudflare, and Google. Provider processing is not a promise that every copy stays on your iPhone or in one country. The competition database is configured in the U.S. West region; this does not establish the location of all provider processing.

HealthComp's beta does not display advertising, sell participant data, or track participants across other companies' apps or websites. These commitments do not mean that hosting and service providers collect no operational diagnostics.

Controls, deletion, and retained history

You can change HealthComp's Health access and notification permissions through Apple's system controls. Withdrawing access stops the corresponding future access; it does not itself delete previously synchronized competition records. If you choose Save Image for a result image, iOS asks for add-only Photos access, which you can change in Apple's system controls.

You can block someone from a challenge they sent you or from their page in Friends. A blocked person can't challenge you or find you in search, and you stop being friends. Blocks are stored with your account, aren’t shown to the blocked person, and are removed when either account is deleted.

In HealthComp, tap your profile avatar on the Home screen to open account settings. Sign Out and Delete Account are different: signing out retires this installation and removes this profile's local competition cache after confirmed retirement; it does not delete the account or shared history. A paired Apple Watch clears its copy the next time it connects as your active Watch.

Delete Account starts an authenticated cleanup workflow. When it completes, Apple authorization is revoked, the service authentication identity is removed, one-on-one matches and group matches that have not started are cancelled, and in a group match that has started your days so far are kept while the others play on. Your username, friends and friend requests, your profile photo, and the photo and account reports made by or about you are deleted, and this profile's local data is cleared. A paired Apple Watch clears its copy the next time it connects as your active Watch. Completed shared results remain for the other participants as Former competitor. The service removes the account linkage and replaces display names in the protected competition history. Revoked installations lose their push tokens; related integrity keys/challenges are purged by the cleanup hooks.

Shared derived results, reactions you sent, stable history identifiers, and some operational records remain. The other participants may already know whom they competed with; the label Former competitor does not guarantee that a person is unidentifiable. Deleting the app, signing out, or starting a deletion request is not confirmation that server cleanup completed. Contact support if deletion cannot finish.

Operational records that remain after the cleanup steps above, and support emails, have no fixed automatic deletion schedule. Account deletion and the preservation of shared competition history work as described above. Service providers' logs and backups are separate from these retained records and follow their own practices. Invitation expiry and installation retirement do not automatically erase every related record. Retained shared competition history also has no fixed automatic deletion schedule. We do not promise that a deletion request immediately erases all provider-held copies or backups.
